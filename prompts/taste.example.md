# Taste file — what this listener actually responds to
#
# Copy to taste.md and start filling it in. This is the file that makes topic selection improve
# instead of drift. Update it EVERY time the listener reacts to an episode, especially when your
# guess was wrong. Record the evidence, not just the conclusion.
#
# The examples below are from the run this kit came from. Replace them with your own; they are
# here to show the shape and the level of honesty that makes the file useful.

## Confirmed preferences (with evidence)

Write one heading per preference. Under each, say what the listener actually said or did — the
evidence — not just your inference from it.

**Example — mechanism, not recap**
> Said explicitly: "I want to understand deeply how they did it and how they thought about it."
> The episode that gave most of its time to method rather than findings produced a long follow-up
> conversation. That is the signal.

**Example — cares most about which claims hold up**
> Every follow-up question was audit-shaped: where does the ground truth come from, why does this
> work, on what grounds does "interpretable" imply "generalizes", how is this different from just
> fitting a network. They found a non-sequitur in the paper themselves, and they were right.
> => Every episode should separate: what the authors proved / what they claimed / how far the
> evidence actually reaches. When something is oversold, say so. Do not defend the paper.

**Example — breadth, and no two consecutive episodes in one field**
> "This is a broad podcast, anything interesting. Don't fixate on one topic."

## Corrections — where I guessed wrong

This section is the most valuable one. Write it up properly when it happens.

**Example — 2026-07-31**
> I inferred from detailed technical follow-ups that they wanted highly technical topics, and
> proposed four. Response: "I don't care about these overly detailed ones. Tomorrow talk about
> this year's Fields Medal. In plain language."
> **Lesson:** those follow-ups were about their own work, not about what they want from the
> podcast. Those are different things. When they hand over material, go deep. For daily topics:
> breadth, accessibility, interest.

**Example — 2026-07-31, second correction**
> The first self-selected topic was well argued and factually solid, and was rejected with
> "not interested". The subject was convergent evolution of leaf shape.
> **Lesson:** a topic can be intellectually elegant and still be a miss. Being clever is not the
> same as being wanted. People, lives, judgement calls and reputations travel further than
> interesting natural phenomena. Ask "would they want to hear about this?" before "is there a
> good argument here?"

## Format

- Target length, and whether overruns were tolerated
- Register: written for the ear, short sentences, plain language
- Quiz style: reasoning rather than recall
- Further reading: note whether each source is free
- Second language, if any: aligned per segment, technical terms left in the original

## Standing constraints

Things the listener has ruled out. Branding, subject areas, tone.
